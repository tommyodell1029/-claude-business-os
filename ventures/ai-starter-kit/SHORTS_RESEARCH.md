# AI Side Hustle Starter Kit: what Shorts viewers watch (2026-10-11)

Source: Social Radar Shorts sweep, 2026-10-11 7:37 PM ET (official YouTube Data API; 8 searches, US, English, last 30 days; 132 Shorts of 3 minutes or less; 804 quota units, $0.006). All numbers are YouTube's own counts at sweep time. Views are not sales: nothing here shows that anyone earned money.

## What the data shows

1. **Skits beat tutorials.** The most-watched AI Shorts are short comedic scenes, not lessons. Examples: "Breaking Bad But Walter Doesn't Make Much Money" (2.9M views in 21 days, 1.2k comments) and "Bro can't keep doing this" (1.6M in 23 days, 1.5k comments). About 25 of the 132 Shorts follow the same skit pattern ("he's onto something", "W professor", "bro can't keep doing this"). Most of those are tagged #higgsfieldpartner, which means they are **paid partner content**. Their reach shows that the format works, not that organic demand exists. This is the same animated story format we already post.
2. **Prompts get comments.** "3 Claude Prompts for a 20-Day Business Plan" (Sabrina Ramonov) has 32k views and 400 comments, about 1.2% of viewers commenting, the highest rate in the set. "5 Free AI Tools I Use To Run My Business" has 22k views and 280 comments. People ask for the prompts, which points to a prompt-based kit.
3. **"Which AI tools" demand.** "These are the best AI tools for every task TODAY" (Dan Martell): 91k views in 21 days. Several free-tool lists from other channels rank in the middle of the set.
4. **"ChatGPT + X" ideas.** ChatGPT + coloring books, ChatGPT + Roblox, AI art business. These ideas repeat across channels with small view counts (about 4–17k).
5. **Faceless-channel automation is crowded and small.** At least 10 Shorts cover it, mostly promoting tools, and most have under 5k views. Not the lead offer.
6. **Noise to ignore:** dozens of "LOVEABLE AI" #LovablePartner clones (paid partner content, about 1k views each), food business ideas (physical products, outside our lane), and celebrity clips.
